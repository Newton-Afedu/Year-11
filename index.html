Activity 1 · 5 minutes · Think–pair–share

The digital money mystery

You have £100 in your bank account. You transfer £25 to a friend using your banking app. Neither of you touches physical money.

Discuss with a partner

  1. Where does the money exist?
  2. How does your friend's account know they received £25?
  3. What prevents you from sending more money than you have?
  4. Is money in a banking app necessarily cryptocurrency?
Electronic balances are maintained as records by financial institutions. A digital currency exists electronically, but not all electronic payments are cryptocurrencies and not all digital currencies use blockchain.
Activity 2 · 10 minutes · Pair discovery

What counts as digital value?

Digital currency is currency that exists electronically rather than as physical notes or coins. Digital payment and virtual-value systems can work in different ways.

🏦

Bank transfer

A bank records account balances and updates them when a payment is made.

₿

Cryptocurrency

Bitcoin transactions can be recorded on a shared blockchain ledger.

🎮

In-game coins

Virtual tokens can be used to purchase items within a game; they may have limited uses.

Pair investigation

What do the three examples have in common? Which one uses blockchain? What are the benefits and risks of electronic payments?

They all represent value electronically, but operate under different rules. Bitcoin uses a blockchain. Electronic payments can be convenient and fast, but unauthorised access, fraud, outages and lost credentials are risks.
Core concept

What is a blockchain?

A blockchain is a digital ledger containing a time-stamped series of records. Blocks are cryptographically linked, making earlier records difficult to alter without detection.

A ledger is a record of transactions. Each block can contain transactions, a timestamp, its own cryptographic hash and a reference to the previous block's hash.

How is a transaction recorded?

① Request

A user submits a transaction.

② Broadcast

The transaction is shared with the network.

③ Validate

Network participants check the transaction against the rules.

④ Group

Valid transactions are placed into a block.

⑤ Agree

The network's consensus process accepts the block.

⑥ Record

The block joins the chain and the history can be checked.

Activity 4 · 10 minutes · Problem-solving

Blockchain detective

Someone tries to change Alice's payment in Block 1 from 30 coins to 90 coins. Click to investigate what happens to the chain.

Discuss

  1. Why does Block 2 refer to Block 1?
  2. Why would editing Block 1 change its hash?
  3. What is the purpose of a timestamp?
  4. Why is the blockchain useful for tracking transactions?
Blocks refer to earlier hashes to establish cryptographic links. Editing the block changes its hash and causes a mismatch with later references. Timestamps help establish when records were added. The ledger provides a shared, checkable history. A real blockchain also relies on network consensus to resist rewriting history.
